01. Holiday lets: permission first.

Under Berlin’s 2026 updated Zweckentfremdungsverbot guidance, entire main/secondary residences let during your absence generally require permission and a registration number. Self-used secondary homes are generally limited to 90 days/year. Letting up to 49% of your genuine main home differs, but registration is still required. Official rule ↗

02. Ordinary tenancy is not Airbnb.

Long-term rental of a residential apartment is a different legal and financial scenario. Berlin’s rent-brake generally applies to qualifying leases through the end of 2029, with exceptions. Furnishing or setting a 1-month term does not automatically make the rent unrestricted. Official information ↗

03. Budget vacancy and maintenance.

Income is not guaranteed. Premium furnished rental pricing varies by location, finishes, season and legal rights. Ask the building’s WEG for house rules, obtain written local tax/legal advice and budget for bad debt, downtime, insurance, management and actual consumption.

06 / Before a viewing or deposit

Prove the quiet.

The questions for each seller.

  • Is there a residential unit directly above any part of the apartment — and who occupies the space below?
  • What are the actual floor plan, orientation, view from a desk and outdoor sightlines into neighbors?
  • Does the elevator reach the apartment floor? Is the final flight accessible without stairs?
  • Can you show the exact in-unit washer/dryer hook-ups, dishwasher, ventilation and power?
  • What are the most recent WEG annual accounts, Wirtschaftsplan, reserve balance, minutes and approved Sonderumlagen?
  • What does the actual Grundsteuerbescheid say and what is included in Hausgeld?
  • Is the unit truly vacant, free of unregistered tenancies, and permitted for the desired rental use?
  • Can we view the unit at 10 pm and measure indoor noise, footsteps, street noise and summer roof heat?
